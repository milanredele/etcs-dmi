ETCS DMI — the driver's display of the European Train Control System

The Driver Machine Interface (DMI) is the cab display that tells a train driver how fast the train may go, where and how hard it has to brake, which mode and level the on-board system is in, and what lies ahead on the track. This project implements it after the European Union Agency for Railways specification ERA_ERTMS_015560 v4.0.0, in Ada 2012, aimed at small embedded hardware: a 32-bit microcontroller around 200 MHz with 128 kB RAM and 1 MB ROM, no operating system and no libraries beyond the compiler's runtime. Every pixel is drawn by the Ada code into a frame buffer; fonts and symbols are bitmaps compiled into the program.

What runs below is that same Ada code compiled to WebAssembly; nothing is reimplemented in JavaScript. The DMI and a simplified on-board computer (EVC) with a track and train model run as two separate modules that share no memory and exchange the binary messages they would exchange over Ethernet on a train. This page plays everything around them: the display, the touch screen, the loudspeaker, the driver's desk and the cable in between, which you can degrade or cut to see how the DMI copes.

How to try it
  1. Start a mission. On the DMI screen press Main, then Start, and follow the dialogue: a driver ID, Level 1, the train data (length, brake percentage, maximum speed), Yes to validate, and a train running number. Every field takes any number followed by the enter key at the bottom right of the keypad.
  2. Watch the run. The EVC grants a movement authority over a 10 km demo track and the automatic driver sets off: speed restrictions, a neutral section, a level transition you have to acknowledge on the DMI, an unprotected level crossing, a tunnel, and the braking curve down to the end of authority. The strip above the DMI shows where the train is.
  3. Drive yourself. Untick Auto drive and use the throttle/brake slider. Go over the permitted speed to get the warning colours and sounds, further and the EVC brakes for you.
  4. Break the cable. Add latency, jitter, loss or fragmentation under EVC ↔ DMI wire. Tick Cut the link: after one second of silence the DMI discards the picture and shows the system failure symbol; untick and it recovers. Reordering shows what the protocol does not yet survive.
  5. Feed it by hand. Switch the source to the manual EVC panel to put any speed, target, mode or level on the display.

A personal project for learning Ada and for testing how far an AI coding agent can be used in developing reliable software; not certified and not for operational use. Source, specification and tests (GPL-3.0): github.com/milanredele/etcs-dmi.
